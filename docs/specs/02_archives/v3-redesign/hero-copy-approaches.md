## Analysis — why hero taglines go wrong here

**The credibility paradox.** Jérôme's story (robot drummers → AI product
front end) is genuinely unusual. Unusual stories tempt writers into
_announcing_ the unusualness ("versatile", "from X to Y", "the constant is…").
But announced remarkableness reads as bragging; shown remarkableness reads as
fact. The fix: state two facts from the two lives, side by side, flatly. The
reader builds the arc themselves — and a conclusion the reader builds is one
they trust.

**The abstraction ladder.** Every rejected draft climbed it: drummers →
"artistic problems" → "problems" → "craft". Each rung up loses a picture and
gains a pose. The site's own best lines all sit on the bottom rung: poles,
Brest, one serverless endpoint. Hero copy should be written at the altitude
of "robot drummers", never at the altitude of "creative problem-solving".

**Conversational is a rhythm, not a vocabulary.** What makes copy sound
human: short first sentence; a second sentence that reacts to the first
(surprise, correction, understatement); contractions; ending on something
plain rather than a flourish. What makes it sound like marketing: parallel
triads, colons announcing a theme, nouns doing verb work.

**The hero's real job here** is small: make the visitor scroll down with the
thought "ok, this person is interesting AND ships real things". The blog
list and work grid below do the heavy lifting. So the tagline can afford to
be modest — modesty with good facts outperforms confidence with categories.

**One risk to manage:** recruiters skim. Whatever we pick must still contain
the words "web" and something signaling now/product work, so a 3-second skim
lands on the right shelf.

## Loop 1 — five directions (2026-07-19) — ❌ shape rejected (robots led)

Each: draft, why it might work, why it might die.

### D1 — Two facts, no bridge

> I used to build robot drummers that played on street poles.
> Now I build web apps, and write about doing it well.

- Works: pure bottom-rung facts; arc fully implicit; sayable; 24 words.
- Risk: "doing it well" is slightly limp; drummers may steal all attention.

### D2 — The honest inventory

> Robot drummers, an audio editor, a coding kit for schools —
> and these days, web apps. I write about what building them taught me.

- Works: shows range without the word "versatile"; blog hook built in.
- Risk: list rhythm edges back toward the rejected triad cadence.

### D3 — Same hands

> The hands that soldered robot drummers now write TypeScript.
> Different problems, same curiosity.

- Works: physical image, arc in one move, short (16 words).
- Risk: "same curiosity" is one rung up the ladder; poetic-adjacent.

### D4 — Direct address

> You're probably here for the web work — the performance posts,
> the testing guides. Stick around for the robots.

- Works: most conversational of all; flatters the reader's intent; the
  "robots" payoff forces a scroll to Work. Confident without one abstract noun.
- Risk: assumes visitor intent; playful tone must survive on a hiring
  manager's screen. Boldest option.

### D5 — Understatement

> I make software. For ten years it banged on drums in the street;
> now it ships as web apps. The blog covers the second part.

- Works: dry humor ("it banged on drums") in his register; dates vague;
  routes to blog.
- Risk: "I make software" opener is almost too flat; irony may not land
  in a skim.

### Recommendation (loop 1)

**D1** as the safe strong pick, **D4** as the memorable pick. Suggested loop-2
move: Jérôme reacts to all five (kill / keep / remix per draft), we breed the
survivors.
